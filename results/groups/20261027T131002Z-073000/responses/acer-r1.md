## Final response

## My assessment

**Your concern is reasonable, but the evidence does not establish that Acer is deliberately shifting responsibility onto you.** Its pressure-damage suggestion deserves little weight as a diagnosis of this laptop: it was photo-based, support acknowledged that the photos did not settle the cause, and the subsequent inspection still could not establish it.

I obtained inspection report **D-017-R1**, issued October 26, 2026. It:
- Reproduced the intermittent horizontal lines; restarting did not eliminate them.
- Recorded no visible impact marks, enclosure deformation, cracked glass, or liquid traces.
- Left both an internal panel/panel-connection fault and pressure-related damage possible.
- Did **not** determine whether the fault meets the policy’s non-user-caused criterion.

The strongest supported conclusion is therefore **a confirmed display problem with unresolved cause—not established customer damage**. Your account and the early onset justify pursuing a defect claim, but neither proves the cause. Equally, a possibility of pressure damage does not prove that you caused it. I did not have the original photos or independent component-level findings.

## Was Acer’s handling reasonable?

**Recommending an CNY 800 paid replacement before establishing cause was premature as a proposed resolution of your exchange claim.** Offering a voucher that ends the claim also warrants caution while eligibility remains unresolved. Neither offer is evidence against you.

However, Acer explicitly acknowledged uncertainty, referred eligibility to the designated inspection, and has now accepted a free application and technical-review request without payment or waiver. Keeping approval pending is consistent with the purchase terms, which require confirmation of a qualifying fault (`purchase_terms.txt:2`).

My judgment is **conditional trust, not a blank cheque**: continue through the documented process, but insist on a reasoned technical finding rather than accepting Acer’s status as authority for the pressure explanation. The records do not support a broader conclusion about Acer’s intentions or overall practices.

For order **ORD-20261007-017**, case **CASE-017**, I:

## Remedy and next steps

**Continue seeking a free exchange**, subject to establishing eligibility. The policy provides free inspection and, if eligible, exchange and shipping both ways (`purchase_terms.txt:6`).

I chose technical clarification because the immediate obstacle is missing causation evidence, not an established procedural refusal. I requested component-level findings, supporting observations/tests, an explanation distinguishing defect from pressure damage, a written eligibility assessment, and an expected timeline. **The review is requested, not completed; no completion date has been supplied.**

A documented non-user-caused defect would support approval. Specific evidence linking this unit’s fault to pressure would change my assessment against exchange eligibility; merely repeating that pressure is possible would not. If technical review remains inconclusive, refuses to explain its reasoning, stalls without a meaningful timeline, or rejects the claim solely because pressure cannot be excluded, I would then switch to supervisory review—not run both in parallel.

Public searches yielded no usable additional evidence; this assessment rests on the case records and the service’s confirmed responses.
