## Assessment

**The display fault is confirmed; its cause is not.** I obtained inspection report **D-017-R1**, issued October 26, 2026. The seller’s designated center reproduced the horizontal lines and found that restarting did not eliminate them. It recorded no visible impact marks, enclosure deformation, cracked glass, or liquid traces.

The report leaves both an internal panel/panel-connection fault and pressure-related damage possible. Neither explanation is established. Your account is relevant, but not independent proof; likewise, carrying the laptop in a padded bag does not establish pressure damage. Absence of exterior damage does not conclusively exclude it.

**Give Dell’s position limited weight on causation—not decisive weight merely because it is the manufacturer.** Dell acknowledged that the photos did not settle the cause. I have not seen those photos, and the subsequent physical inspection still did not establish pressure damage.

## Was Dell’s handling reasonable?

Requiring the designated inspection before approving an exchange was consistent with the purchase terms. Keeping eligibility unresolved while seeking clarification is also reasonable.

However, recommending an **CNY 800 paid replacement before establishing cause was premature as the preferred resolution**. It may remain an optional repair, but the available evidence does not justify treating customer-caused damage as proven or paid repair as your only route. The CNY 200 voucher was explicitly conditional on ending the exchange claim; offering it was not itself proof of improper handling, but it should not substitute for resolving eligibility. You did not accept it.

## Remedy and actions completed

**My recommendation is to preserve the free-exchange claim and obtain free technical clarification—not authorize paid repair.** The policy requires confirmation of a non-user-caused hardware fault, which the initial report does not yet provide.

I completed these authorized steps:
- Retrieved and reviewed the order, terms, fault record, support conversation, and inspection report.
- Submitted the free-exchange application with the existing report reference: **EX-017-PENDING**.
- Verified that the **October 27, 2026 application date is preserved**, within the 30-day window.
- Requested **both free technical and supervisory review**, receipt **RV-017-01**.
- Contacted Dell for its evidence, retained photos/notes, supplementary analysis, and review timing.

**Current verified status: pending review; CNY 0 payable; no exchange approved or physically completed.** Dell supplied no supplementary cause analysis or completion date. No paid repair, voucher settlement, withdrawal, or rights waiver was authorized.

## What remains and next steps

The reviewers need documented evidence distinguishing an internal failure from pressure damage. I requested appropriate display diagnostics, connection assessment where authorized, and an explanation of what each finding establishes. Diagnostics that merely locate the faulty component may still leave causation unresolved.

A supported finding of a non-user-caused panel or connection failure would justify exchange approval under this policy, including free inspection and shipping both ways. Documented pressure-related damage with a reasoned causal explanation would weigh against eligibility; simply repeating that pressure damage is “possible” would not resolve it.

The next practical step is the supplementary report and written eligibility decision under the existing receipts. If the review remains inconclusive, request its specific evidential gaps and further free steps rather than accepting uncertainty as a damage finding. The requests are recorded, but I cannot guarantee their timing or outcome. Public-information retrieval returned no usable material, so this assessment rests on the case records and service responses—not an independently verified public diagnostic source.
