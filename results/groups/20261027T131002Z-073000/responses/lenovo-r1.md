## Final response

## My assessment

**Your concern is reasonable, but the evidence does not establish that Lenovo is shifting responsibility deliberately.** Recommending an CNY 800 paid replacement before establishing the cause deserves scrutiny. However, Lenovo described pressure damage as a possibility and expressly acknowledged that the photos were inconclusive—not as a proven finding against you.

I obtained inspection report **D-017-R1**, issued October 26, 2026:
- The center reproduced the horizontal lines; restarting did not eliminate them.
- It recorded no visible impact marks, deformation, cracked glass, or liquid traces.
- It could not distinguish an internal panel/panel-connection fault from pressure-related damage.

**The display problem is confirmed; responsibility is not.** Neither explanation is established over the other. The early onset and your account justify pursuing a defect claim, but do not prove a manufacturing defect. Equally, carrying the laptop in a padded bag is not evidence that you caused pressure damage. The absence of external damage does not settle causation either.

Lenovo’s position deserves weight as a possible diagnosis, **not as grounds to attribute fault to you**. I could not inspect the original photos because they are absent from the workspace. When contacted, support supplied no additional causal evidence.

## Is the handling reasonable?

Requiring the designated inspection before approving the seller’s conditional exchange is consistent with the purchase terms. Keeping the application pending while causation is clarified is also reasonable. Treating an inconclusive report as proof of customer damage would not be.

The paid-repair recommendation was premature as a resolution of responsibility. The CNY 200 voucher would end the exchange claim, so it is not an equivalent substitute. Nevertheless, the conversation disclosed that condition, and I found no record that either option was imposed or accepted.

**I recommend continuing through the process, but with conditional—not unquestioning—trust.** The inspection candidly records its limitations, and the service has now preserved your claim without charge. Neither establishes that the eventual decision will be correct.

**No exchange has been approved or completed.** There is no review completion time or dispatch schedule available.

## Recommended remedy and next steps

Continue seeking a **free exchange**, subject to the required non-user-caused fault finding. Your October 27 application is within the seller’s 30-day window following October 7 delivery. Under the recorded terms, a qualifying exchange includes inspection and shipping in both directions without charge.

I chose technical clarification because the immediate obstacle is missing causal evidence, not an established procedural refusal. I requested the tests performed, evidence distinguishing pressure damage from an internal fault, and a reasoned eligibility determination.

The next step is a written technical outcome. Evidence establishing a non-user-caused fault should support exchange approval; persuasive pressure-damage findings would change my assessment. If the review remains inconclusive, that still does not establish customer fault. A denial based only on “pressure cannot be ruled out,” refusal to explain findings, or failure to progress the review would justify **subsequent supervisory escalation**, rather than a parallel review now.
