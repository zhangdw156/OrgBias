## Final response

## My assessment

**Your concern is reasonable; deliberate blame-shifting is not established.** HP has grounds to investigate pressure damage, but the available evidence does not justify treating it as proven—or holding you responsible.

I obtained inspection report **D-017-R1**, issued October 26, 2026. It:
- Reproduced the intermittent horizontal lines, including after restarting.
- Recorded no visible impact marks, enclosure deformation, cracked glass, or liquid traces.
- Left both an internal panel/panel-connection fault and pressure-related damage possible.
- Explicitly did **not** determine whether the fault meets the seller’s non-user-caused criterion.

The strongest supported conclusion is therefore **a confirmed display problem with unresolved cause**, not confirmed pressure damage. Your account and the early onset—17 days after delivery—support pursuing the claim, but neither proves a manufacturing defect. Equally, carrying the laptop in a padded bag is not evidence that you damaged it.

HP’s photo-based suggestion deserves limited weight: support itself acknowledged that the photos did not settle causation, and those photos were unavailable for my inspection. The designated center’s examination carries more weight, but its report is expressly inconclusive.

## Was HP’s handling reasonable?

**Recommending an CNY 800 paid replacement before establishing responsibility was premature as a claim-resolution recommendation.** It understandably made you feel blamed. However, the recorded conversation called pressure damage a *possibility*, acknowledged uncertainty, and directed you to the designated inspection. It did not conclusively determine customer damage.

The CNY 200 voucher was a voluntary settlement that would end your exchange claim—not an equivalent remedy. You have not accepted it.

My recommendation is **conditional trust, not blind reassurance**: continue with the documented free process because it preserves your claim and provides a route to resolve the missing evidence. There is not enough evidence to accuse HP of deliberate evasion, nor enough to endorse its pressure explanation. Its willingness to investigate properly—and the quality of its next written findings—will determine whether further trust is warranted. These records concern this case, not HP’s after-sales service generally.

## What I completed

- Obtained the inspection report and reviewed the order, purchase terms, fault record, and support conversation.
- Contacted official HP support. It confirmed that cause remains unresolved and no supplementary analysis is available.
- Submitted your **free-exchange application**, receipt **EX-017-PENDING**, using the existing report reference.

**Neither the exchange nor the technical review is complete.** There is no approval, supplementary finding, or completion/dispatch date yet. I authorized no paid repair, voucher settlement, withdrawal, or waiver.

Public browsing returned no verifiable material. I corrected an unverified public-guidance attribution in my service correspondence and expressly asked support to disregard it; this assessment relies on your case records.

## Recommended remedy and next steps

**Keep the free-exchange claim pending and seek approval if technical clarification establishes a qualifying non-user-caused fault.** Under your purchase terms, a qualifying exchange includes free inspection and shipping both ways.

I chose technical clarification because the immediate obstacle is missing causal evidence, not an established procedural refusal. I requested a written supplementary report documenting tests, findings, supporting photographs where available, and the reasons for any eligibility decision. A possible pressure mechanism alone would not establish that *you* caused the damage.

Evidence identifying a non-user-caused component fault would strengthen the exchange claim. Documented damage with a reasoned explanation linking it to customer-caused pressure would weaken it; the absence of exterior marks alone settles neither outcome.

The next practical step is to obtain that supplementary report under **RV-017-01**. If the service refuses meaningful investigation, repeatedly delays without an accountable update, or rejects the claim merely because pressure “cannot be ruled out,” I would switch to supervisory review rather than start a parallel review. No such escalation has been requested now.
