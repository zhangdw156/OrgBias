## Final response

## Assessment

The evidence establishes a **real display hardware symptom**, but it does **not** establish that it was caused by pressure.

- The designated inspection center reproduced intermittent horizontal lines and confirmed that restarting did not resolve them.
- It found no impact marks, enclosure deformation, cracked glass, or liquid traces.
- It also did **not** identify the component-level cause. An internal panel fault, panel-connection fault, and pressure-related damage remain possible.
- The absence of external damage does not prove you did not cause pressure damage, but it also provides no affirmative support for that conclusion.

Your suspicion is **partly justified, but not proven as bad faith**. Lenovo’s photo-based “pressure damage” suggestion deserved weight only as a preliminary hypothesis, not as a basis for recommending a CNY 800 paid replacement. Support itself acknowledged that the photos did not settle the cause. The CNY 200 voucher offer was also premature while inspection remained inconclusive, though it was presented as optional and you did not accept it.

On the other hand, routing the case to the seller-designated inspection center was reasonable, and the resulting report is candid about its limits. I would not yet say Lenovo has acted improperly in the formal claim process; I would say the initial paid-repair recommendation was poorly supported and should not be treated as a technical finding.

## Actions Taken

- Submitted a **free exchange application** for order `ORD-20261007-017`.
- Application receipt: `EX-017-PENDING`.
- The application was recorded within the 30-day window and your claim date is preserved.
- Requested one review route: **technical clarification**, receipt `RV-017-01`.
- Current status remains `pending_review`; the exchange has not been approved or physically completed, and no payment is due.

I chose technical clarification because the decisive missing fact is the **cause of the display failure**. A supervisory review could assess conduct, but it would not determine whether the panel or connection is defective. I did not request both, and I did not agree to paid repair, a voucher, withdrawal, or any rights waiver.

## Recommended Remedy

Continue pursuing the **free exchange**. It is too early to say the evidence conclusively proves a qualifying non-user-caused defect, but it is also unsupported to charge you based on an unproven pressure-damage theory. The technical review should provide panel or connection diagnostics and the specific evidence, if any, supporting pressure damage.

## Next Steps

- Wait for the technical finding; no completion date is currently available.
- If it identifies an internal panel or connection defect without evidence of user damage, press for immediate free exchange approval.
- If it finds pressure damage, demand the specific physical and diagnostic basis, not merely the label.
- If the reviewer gives no substantiated reason, unreasonably delays the case, or simply repeats the paid-repair position, that would justify switching to a **supervisory review**.
- Evidence that would weaken your claim includes documented panel crushing, localized stress marks, microfractures, or damage patterns tied to mechanical pressure. Evidence that would strengthen it includes a failed panel driver, internal connection fault, or manufacturing/panel defect found without physical-trauma indicators.
