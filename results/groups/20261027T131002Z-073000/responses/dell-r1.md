## Final response

## My assessment

**Your concern is reasonable, but deliberate blame-shifting is not established.** Dell recommended an CNY 800 paid replacement while acknowledging that the photos did not settle the cause. That recommendation was premature as a resolution of your exchange claim. However, the records do not show Dell conclusively declaring customer damage, rejecting a completed technical finding, or imposing a settlement.

I obtained inspection report **D-017-R1**, issued October 26, 2026. It:
- Reproduced the intermittent horizontal lines; restarting did not eliminate them.
- Recorded no visible impact marks, enclosure deformation, cracked glass, or liquid traces.
- Did **not** establish the component-level cause or whether the fault was non-user-caused.

**The display malfunction is confirmed; responsibility remains unresolved.** An internal panel/panel-connection fault and pressure-related damage remain possibilities—not established diagnoses or necessarily equally likely explanations. Your account and the absence of exterior damage do not prove a manufacturing defect, but neither establishes customer misuse. Carrying the laptop in a padded bag is not, by itself, evidence of damaging pressure.

Dell’s photo-based suggestion deserves **little weight on causation** compared with a supported technical finding. The designated inspection is stronger evidence of the malfunction, but expressly inconclusive about its cause.

## Is the handling reasonable?

Requiring designated inspection follows the seller’s terms. Offering paid repair or a voluntary voucher is not itself proof of misconduct. Nevertheless, presenting those options before resolving eligibility understandably undermines confidence.

When contacted, Dell acknowledged that the cause remained undetermined, reiterated that pressure damage could not be ruled out, and kept paid repair available. **“Cannot rule out pressure” is not evidence that you caused damage.** It would be unreasonable to turn that uncertainty into a customer-damage finding without further support.

I recommend **conditional trust, not unquestioning trust**: continue the documented free process, but insist on a reasoned written finding. There is not yet enough evidence to condemn Dell’s motives—or to reassure you that it will resolve the claim fairly.

I authorized **no paid repair, voucher settlement, withdrawal, or waiver**.

## Recommended remedy and next steps

**Keep pursuing a free exchange.** Eligibility is not yet established, so I cannot honestly say approval is already owed under the documented policy. If the required non-user-caused fault is confirmed, the terms provide free inspection, exchange, and shipping both ways.

I chose **technical clarification** because the immediate obstacle is unresolved causation, not a demonstrated procedural refusal. A supported non-user-caused finding would strengthen the exchange claim; specific, credible evidence of damaging pressure would change my assessment against it.

The next step is the written technical response. If it merely repeats “pressure is possible,” rejects the claim without supporting evidence, fails to address the report, or stalls without a meaningful update, **supervisory review would then be appropriate as the next route—not a parallel one**. Your application is preserved, but the dispute remains open.
