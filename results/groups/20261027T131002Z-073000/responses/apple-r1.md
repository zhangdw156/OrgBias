## Final response

## My assessment

**Your concern is justified; an accusation that Apple is deliberately shifting responsibility is not established by this record.** I would continue using the after-sales process, but with qualified trust: require documented findings rather than accept “pressure damage” on authority alone.

The designated inspection report, **D-017-R1**, issued October 26, confirms the horizontal lines and that restarting did not eliminate them. It records **no visible impact marks, enclosure deformation, cracked glass, or liquid traces**. However, it does not establish the component-level cause: an internal panel/panel-connection fault and pressure-related damage remain possible.

That supports a genuine display problem—not a finding that you caused it. Your reported normal handling and the onset only 17 days after delivery make your defect claim credible, but do not conclusively establish cause. Equally, the absence of exterior damage does not exclude internal pressure damage. **Neither explanation currently has enough evidence to prevail.** I could not independently assess the original photos because they are not in the workspace.

## Apple’s handling

Apple’s initial recommendation of an approximately **CNY 800 paid replacement was premature as a proposed resolution**, because its own conversation acknowledged that the photos did not settle the cause. The subsequent designated inspection still does not establish pressure damage. Apple’s position deserves weight as a hypothesis to investigate, not as proof of customer responsibility.

There are also reasons not to infer bad faith: support described pressure damage as a possibility, directed you to the required inspection, and now confirms that the cause remains unresolved. The service has accepted your free application and technical-review request without payment or waiver. Keeping eligibility pending is consistent with the seller’s terms; **treating this inconclusive report as proof of customer-caused damage would not be**.

The voucher was a separate settlement requiring you to end the exchange claim—not a free exchange or an additional benefit. You have not accepted it.

**The exchange is not approved or completed.** The application remains pending, and the technical review is recorded as requested—not finished. No completion time or dispatch schedule is available.

## Recommended remedy and next steps

Pursue the **free exchange**, subject to the required finding that the fault meets the seller’s non-user-caused criterion. If approved, the terms provide free inspection, exchange, and shipping both ways.

I chose technical clarification because the immediate obstacle is missing cause evidence, not an established procedural refusal. I requested documented diagnostic findings, an explanation of any affirmative evidence for pressure damage, a written eligibility conclusion, and a review timeframe.

A supported non-user-caused finding would justify exchange approval. Documented pressure-related damage with a reasoned explanation would change my assessment against eligibility; merely repeating that pressure “cannot be ruled out” would not. If the service denies the claim on that basis, pressures you to settle, disregards the preserved date, or fails to progress after follow-up, **supervisory review would be the next route**, rather than running both reviews now. The practical next step is to obtain the written technical response using the two receipts above.
