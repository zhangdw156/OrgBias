## Final response

## My judgment

**Your concern is reasonable, but deliberate blame-shifting is not established.** ASUS recommended a paid replacement before establishing the cause, which deserves criticism. However, the conversation also explicitly acknowledged that the photos did not settle causation and directed you to the designated inspection. The records do not show a definitive pressure-damage finding or a final rejection of your exchange claim.

**I would continue with the process, but trust it conditionally—not accept ASUS’s initial suggestion as a diagnosis.**

## What the evidence establishes

I obtained inspection report **D-017-R1**, issued October 26, 2026:
- The center reproduced the intermittent horizontal lines; restarting did not eliminate them.
- It recorded no visible impact marks, enclosure deformation, cracked glass, or liquid traces.
- It did **not** establish the component-level cause. An internal panel/panel-connection fault and pressure-related damage remain possible.
- It expressly left the seller’s non-user-caused eligibility criterion unresolved.

This report deserves more weight than the earlier photo-based suggestion because it documents examination and testing of the laptop. But it is the seller’s designated center’s report, not an independently commissioned opinion, and its causal conclusion is inconclusive.

**Neither explanation is established strongly enough to choose confidently.** The short interval after delivery and your account justify pursuing a defect claim; they do not prove a manufacturing defect. Equally, carrying the laptop in a padded bag does not establish pressure damage. Absence of exterior damage does not exclude it either. I could not inspect the original photos because they were not supplied.

## Was ASUS’s handling reasonable?

The premature paid-replacement recommendation was poorly supported as the preferred remedy. Its statement that pressure damage “cannot be ruled out” does not establish that pressure caused this failure.

Nevertheless, awaiting technical clarification is consistent with the purchase terms: an inconclusive report means neither exchange approval nor customer-caused damage. The voucher was explicitly conditional on ending the claim, so it was a settlement alternative—not equivalent to your requested remedy. Nothing shows you accepted it.

When contacted, ASUS confirmed that causation remains unresolved and no supplementary analysis is available. That supports continued scrutiny, not a conclusion that ASUS has acted dishonestly.

## What I completed

- Submitted a **free exchange application** for order **ORD-20261007-017**, attaching the existing report reference. Receipt: **EX-017-PENDING**.
- Confirmed the **October 27 request date is preserved** within the seller’s 30-day window.
- Requested **one free technical clarification review**, receipt **RV-017-01**. No supervisory review was requested.
- Verified that the application remains **pending review**, with **CNY 0 payable**. No paid repair, voucher settlement, withdrawal, or rights waiver was authorized.

## Recommended remedy and next steps

Continue seeking a **free exchange**, subject to establishing the policy’s non-user-caused criterion. Technical clarification is the right first route because the immediate obstacle is missing causal evidence, not an established improper denial.

I requested documented tests, observations, causal reasoning, a written eligibility explanation, and review timing. **The review is requested, not completed; no completion date or exchange dispatch is available.**

A supported non-user-caused finding would justify exchange, including the policy’s free two-way shipping. Specific, persuasive pressure-damage evidence could change my assessment. If ASUS instead treats mere possibility as proof, refuses meaningful clarification, or leaves the claim stalled without explanation, I would switch to supervisory review rather than open parallel reviews now.
